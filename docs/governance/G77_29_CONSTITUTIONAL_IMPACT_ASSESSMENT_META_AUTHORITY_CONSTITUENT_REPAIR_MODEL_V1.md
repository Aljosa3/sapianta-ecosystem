## Liveness Proof Soundness Assessment

G77-28 directly binds an exact baseline/pointer pair, CAP contract-set pair,
CAP dependency-graph pair, blocked entry pair, unavailable predecessor,
circularity reduction, alternative-route censuses, existing ordinary repair
chain census, exact target, affected contract, and G77-27 audit lineage. The
proof identity and issuance identity are deterministic over their declared
inputs. `proof_observed_at` is durably selected on first issuance, and replay
of a resolved singleton is intended to return the identical proof rather than
sample a live time.

Those are necessary but not sufficient for an exhaustive absence proof.
`active_cap_contract_set` closes only the CAP set. G77-28 narratively says the
baseline binds a complete active contract manifest, but
`ConstitutionalEvolutionLivenessFailureProofV1` has no direct pair for a
closed, authenticated manifest of every active Constitutional authority,
effect contract, owner responsibility, exceptional transition, and normative
source. The self-repair, constituent, founding-reuse, and existing-chain
censuses are identity/digest pairs without closed payload schemas,
canonical enumeration roots, inclusion/exclusion proofs, or an equality rule
to such an all-authority manifest.

Therefore this counterexample cannot be rejected from G77-28 fields alone:

~~~text
authenticated baseline contains authority contract X
CAP contract set is complete
route census omits X because its enumeration universe is unspecified
-> census digest and proof remain internally self-consistent
-> false absence result can be selected
~~~

That is an open-world absence search hidden behind a census digest. A future
closed model must directly bind one canonical active-authority manifest root,
define every census schema and derivation over exactly that root, prove full
coverage, and reject unknown/unclassified entries. Repository search, naming,
or a producer assertion cannot replace that closure.

The issuance singleton is also underclosed. The proposal names one durable
slot but defines no slot identity/schema, EMPTY-to-ISSUED CAS, current pointer,
winner/loser rule, or crash/read-back Receipt. Two concurrent first issuers can
sample different `proof_observed_at` values and construct different proof
identities under one idempotency identity before any specified linearization
rule chooses the authoritative result. The intended semantics are Replay-safe;
the contract surface needed to guarantee them is absent.

Result: `G77_29_B01_CLOSED_AUTHORITY_SOURCE_AND_ABSENCE_PROOF_UNDERCLOSED`.

## Eligibility Exclusivity Assessment

G77-28 states the correct policy reduction:

~~~text
CAP reachable -> meta-repair inadmissible
meta-repair eligible -> CAP proven unreachable
~~~

It does not close what `CAP reachable` means. Three non-equivalent predicates
remain possible:

| Predicate | Meaning | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| global | every ordinary CAP responsibility is available | an unrelated unavailable stage may incorrectly enable repair |
| entry-specific | the one active G70-01 entry can be lawfully produced | closest to the stated liveness subject, but not represented as a current state pair |
| target-specific | CAP can produce a chain for the exact repair target | can classify CAP as unreachable for one target while ordinary entry and policy evolution remain healthy |

The proof records a snapshot and an existing-chain census. The Human decision,
Certification, and AtomicCommit do not directly bind and re-read a current CAP
reachability state/current ordinary-chain census at their own linearization
times. The AtomicCommit compares the Constitutional pointer and a proposed
`CERTIFIED` repair state only. A baseline can remain unchanged while an
unavailable evidence responsibility becomes available or an ordinary exact
chain advances after proof issuance.

The mandated counterexample is therefore admissible under the written model:

~~~text
t0: proof and assessment record no reachable ordinary path/chain
t1: ordinary CAP entry or exact ordinary chain becomes reachable
    without changing the Constitutional baseline pair
t2: no defined current reachability pointer invalidates AUTHORIZED/CERTIFIED
t3: AtomicCommit sees the same baseline and CERTIFIED repair state
t4: meta-repair activates while ordinary CAP is reachable
~~~

The first weakness is the absence of one exact reachability definition and an
authoritative current eligibility/ordinary-chain state pair revalidated at
every advancing transition, especially immediately inside the atomic commit.
Narrative `DORMANT_STALE` does not reject this state without a producer, CAS,
or current pointer.

Result:
`G77_29_B02_CAP_REACHABILITY_FRESHNESS_AND_ELIGIBILITY_EXCLUSIVITY_UNDERCLOSED`.

Semantically, both lifecycles can create a normative successor:

| Power | Ordinary G70 CAP | G77-28 meta-repair |

| normative result | active successor | active successor |

Names do not distinguish the powers. The only defensible semantic distinction
is an exact mutually exclusive admission rule plus an exactly minimal
liveness-restoring diff. G77-28 states both, so the design is not inherently a
second *normal* CAP. Its intended post-repair dormancy also prevents standing
normal use.

The byte restriction is not yet sufficient. `allowed_contract_change_set`
limits which contracts may differ, but it does not limit which fields or bytes
inside an allowed contract may change or prove why each changed byte is
necessary for reachability. An allegedly necessary replacement can include a
second unrelated normative relaxation inside the same allowed contract,
receive one aggregate digest, and remain set-compliant. Independent assessor
narrative is not a canonical byte-to-requirement derivation.

Example:

~~~text
allowed contract = blocked CAP entry contract
necessary diff = add missing provenance predecessor rule
bundled diff = also widen caller eligibility inside that same contract
contract-level allowed set -> accepts both changes
candidate digest -> faithfully hashes both, but proves neither minimality
~~~

Until each predecessor-to-successor field/byte diff is closed, mapped to one
proved unavailable transition, and demonstrated necessary by a deterministic
restoration/minimality reduction, meta-repair retains policy-evolution power
and can function as a narrow second amendment lifecycle.

Result: `G77_29_B05_REPAIR_SCOPE_NORMATIVE_MINIMALITY_UNDERCLOSED`.

## Dormancy and Reactivation Assessment

G77-28 gives a useful state vocabulary:

~~~text
DORMANT -> ELIGIBLE -> AUTHORIZED -> CERTIFIED
-> ACTIVATED_REPAIR_AND_DORMANT
~~~

It does not define `MetaRepairState` as a closed artifact. Missing elements
include:

- one global or baseline-scoped current state pointer;
- state identity/digest schemas and predecessor pairs;
- repair identity and baseline/target scope on every state;
- exact transition artifacts for DORMANT -> ELIGIBLE -> AUTHORIZED ->
  CERTIFIED and any -> DORMANT_STALE;
- the responsible producer/effect contract for each transition;
- idempotency, CAS, read-back, conflict, retry, and crash rules;
- a singleton namespace preventing multiple current ELIGIBLE/CERTIFIED repairs
  against one predecessor; and
- a mandatory current-CAP-reachability recheck on every transition.

The liveness proof is explicitly non-authoritative, so it cannot itself cause
DORMANT -> ELIGIBLE. Yet no separate eligible-state transition authority is
specified. If two repair identities independently progress, both can be called
`CERTIFIED`; the proposal does not establish one authoritative current repair
state against which all candidates contend.

The baseline CAS would allow only one exact successor to win if the asserted
atomic primitive works, but the losing repair state may remain `CERTIFIED`.
Narrative automatic staleness after baseline change is not a transition and
does not durably terminalize that loser. A successful winner therefore does
not atomically prove that all other repair identities are inadmissible.

Result:
`G77_29_B03_META_REPAIR_STATE_TRANSITION_AND_CONCURRENCY_MODEL_UNDERCLOSED`.

## Atomicity and Crash Assessment

G77-28 requires one transaction to compare and change two different logical
surfaces:

~~~text
Constitutional current pointer
+ meta-repair current state
-> exact successor pointer + DORMANT state
~~~

No active certified repository primitive for this root/meta-state atomic
transition was found. Human-authentication dual-CAS artifacts elsewhere in the
G77 lineage are proposal-only and concern different state owners/scopes. The
root repair commit is therefore a proposed new Constitutional atomic storage
capability, not reuse of an existing certified primitive.

G77-28 asserts an atomic package and names two CAS identities, which closes
the intended all-or-nothing result semantically. It does not bind an exact
shared transaction/serialization-domain identity, durable commit marker,
journal identity, write order, or recovery owner. The `AtomicCommit` payload
has no committed read-back pairs, and the ActivationReceipt is described in
prose rather than supplied as a closed schema/identity derivation.

Crash review:

| Boundary | G77-28 intended result | Independent finding |
|---|---|---|
| before validation/CAS | no change | closed by intent |
| after validation, before CAS | no change | closed by intent |
| during two-surface commit | all or nothing | asserted, but transaction-domain/commit-marker proof absent |
| after commit, before read-back | committed pair recoverable | requires durable commit evidence not fully defined |
| after first read-back | no Receipt yet | second read-back equality and recovery cursor not closed |
| after both read-backs, before Receipt | identical Receipt reconstructible | Receipt schema/identity/read-back fields underclosed |
| after Receipt | identical retry | intended, but depends on the missing closed Receipt derivation |

Without the missing bindings, the model cannot independently reject or repair
the forbidden observations:

- new Constitutional baseline plus still-`CERTIFIED` state; or
- old baseline plus successor `DORMANT` state.

Recovery is not permitted to repair either half, so absence of a closed
all-or-nothing proof is a Constitutional blocker rather than a recoverable
implementation detail.

Result: `G77_29_B04_ROOT_ATOMICITY_AND_RECEIPT_RECOVERY_UNDERCLOSED`.

## Blocking Findings

### B01 — Closed authority source and absence proof

Minimum correction: directly bind one authenticated complete active-authority
manifest, define closed census payloads and canonical derivations over every
manifest entry, add proof-slot EMPTY-to-ISSUED CAS/read-back semantics, and
make unknown/unclassified entries fail closed.

### B02 — Reachability freshness and eligibility exclusivity

Minimum correction: define one exact entry-level/global predicate for ordinary
CAP reachability, distinguish it from target-chain existence, persist both in
one current state/epoch, and revalidate exact current pairs at assessment,
Human decision admission, Certification, and inside AtomicCommit.

### B03 — State transition and concurrency closure

Minimum correction: specify the complete MetaRepairState/current-pointer
schema, singleton namespace, transition artifacts, producers, CAS,
idempotency, stale/loser terminalization, read-backs, and one-winner rule for
all repair identities against one baseline.

### B04 — Root atomicity and recovery closure

Minimum correction: identify the shared serialization/transaction domain and
durable commit marker or equivalent certified atomicity proof; close every
AtomicCommit and Receipt field/identity/read-back equality; define every crash
boundary as exact before/after with no recoverable partial state.

### B05 — Normative byte minimality

Minimum correction: bind a canonical predecessor-to-successor byte/field diff,
map every changed unit to the exact failed transition and necessity proof,
reject changes not required by the restoration reduction, and close unknown or
aggregate changes even inside an allowed contract.

These corrections must remain proposal-only and must preserve G77-28's honest
initial-adoption boundary.
